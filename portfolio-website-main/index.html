Guiding Philosophy

My Principles

Three timeless pillars of righteous living, honest work, and community sharing

My work, engineering journey, and this student resource initiative are guided by the three core principles taught by Guru Nanak Dev Ji. Among these, Vand Chhako is the foundation of why these electronics and materials are freely shared.

Pillar I

Kirat Karo

ਕਿਰਤ ਕਰੋ • Honest Labor & Sincere Effort

Earn an honest livelihood through earnest effort, dedication, integrity, and hard work. As engineers, innovators, and students, our work should be rooted in true technical craftsmanship and the relentless pursuit of solving genuine human problems without shortcuts.

Living the Principle: Working hard to build real hardware startups, publish rigorous research, and create value through sincere sweat and discipline.
Pillar II

Naam Japo

ਨਾਮ ਜਪੋ • Mindful Remembrance & Humility

Keep the Divine and ultimate truth at the forefront of every action. Stay grounded with a humble mind, free from pride and ego. Knowledge and intellect are blessings meant to enlighten ourselves and bring compassion to how we treat everyone around us.

Living the Principle: Remembering that our abilities are gifts, and using engineering intellect with humility to serve society rather than personal vanity.
Pillar III • The Core of This Initiative

Vand Chhako

ਵੰਡ ਛਕੋ • Selfless Sharing with the Community

Share what you earn, your knowledge, your blessings, and your resources with those in need before consuming for yourself. No single person prospers alone; true wealth is created when we uplift the entire collective community.

Connecting to Student Resources: Hardware components sitting locked in boxes create zero innovation. By sharing microcontrollers, sensors, ICs, and lab tools freely with students who need them, we ensure lack of money never stops a curious student from building.

The Mission & Heart

Why I Do This

As an Electronics and Communication Engineering student at the Faculty of Technology, University of Delhi, and the founder of PrintRocket, I have spent countless late nights building circuits, soldering prototypes, programming microcontrollers, and debugging hardware.

Time and again, I have seen brilliant, passionate student engineers hesitate or give up on an idea simply because buying an Arduino board, a handful of sensors, a motor driver, or a multimeter strained their pocket money. In hardware engineering, components add up quickly—and for a student with tight finances, that barrier can feel impossible.

"If a student has the willingness to learn, lack of money should never be the reason they stop building."

Rooted in the Sikh principle of Vand Chhako (sharing with the community), I believe components sitting in drawers or on shelves produce zero value. But when put into the eager hands of a student builder, they turn into robots, prototypes, research publications, and career breakthroughs. That is why these materials are free.
